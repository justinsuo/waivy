#### 🍪 [Classic Shortbread Cookies](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake-shortbread-cookies/)
Scottish · snack · easy  
**$0.38/serving** · 210 kcal · P2 · C16 · F15 · ⏱ 75 min · 🍽 12  

Buttery, crumbly Scottish-style shortbread with just five ingredients. A high ratio of real butter and a touch of vanilla give these a melt-in-your-mouth snap and golden, sandy edges — no leavening, no eggs, just the classic 3:2:1 method done right.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Butter** — 1 cup (226g / 2 sticks) unsalted, softened but still cool
- **All-purpose flour** — 2 cups (240g) all-purpose flour
- **Powdered sugar** — 3/4 cup (90g) powdered sugar for a finer, tender crumb
- **Vanilla extract** — 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
- **Salt** — 1/4 tsp fine salt

**Steps**

1. Cream the softened butter, powdered sugar, vanilla, and salt together with a wooden spoon or mixer for 2-3 minutes until pale, light, and fluffy — this aeration is what gives shortbread its tender texture.
2. Add the flour all at once and mix on low (or fold by hand) just until the dough comes together into a soft, slightly crumbly mass. Stop the moment no dry flour remains — overmixing develops gluten and makes the cookies tough.
3. Gather the dough, press it into a disc, wrap, and chill for at least 30 minutes so the butter firms up and the cookies hold their shape.
4. Preheat the oven to 325F (160C) and line a baking sheet with parchment. A lower temperature keeps the shortbread pale and lets it dry out into that signature sandy crumb.
5. Roll the chilled dough to about 1/3 inch thick on a lightly floured surface and cut into rounds or fingers. Prick each cookie a few times with a fork for the traditional look and even baking.
6. Transfer cookies to the lined sheet, spacing them 1 inch apart, and chill on the tray for 10 minutes so they keep crisp edges in the oven.
7. Bake for 16-20 minutes, until the edges are just barely golden and the tops are set but still pale. Do not let them brown all over.
8. Let the cookies cool on the tray for 5 minutes to firm up, then transfer to a rack. Optionally dust with a little extra sugar while warm.

</details>

#### 🍪 [Empire Biscuits](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake2-empire-biscuits/)
Scottish · snack · medium  
**$0.62/serving** · 322 kcal · P3 · C37 · F18 · ⏱ 95 min · 🍽 10  

The classic Scottish teatime treat: two rounds of crisp, buttery shortbread-style biscuit sandwiched with raspberry jam, blanketed in smooth white water icing, and crowned with a single glacé cherry. Tender, sweet, and nostalgic — the kind that vanishes off the plate at any church hall or gran's kitchen table.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **All-purpose flour** — 2 cups all-purpose flour, plus extra for dusting
- **Butter** — 1 cup (2 sticks / 225g) unsalted butter, softened
- **Sugar** — 1/2 cup caster (superfine) sugar
- **Vanilla extract** — 1 tsp vanilla extract
- **Salt** — 1/4 tsp fine salt
- **Jam** — about 6 tbsp seedless raspberry jam, for sandwiching
- **Powdered sugar** — 1 1/2 cups (about 175g) powdered/icing sugar, for the water icing
- **Water** — about 3 tbsp water, added a little at a time to the icing
- **Fresh cherries** — 10 glacé cherries, halved (one half per biscuit) — or use jelly tots _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. Beat the softened butter and caster sugar together until pale, light, and fluffy, 3-4 minutes. Beat in the vanilla. Add the flour and salt and mix on low just until the dough comes together into a soft, smooth ball — do not overwork it. Flatten into a disc, wrap, and chill for 30 minutes to firm up.
2. Preheat the oven to 350F (175C) and line two baking sheets with parchment. On a lightly floured surface, roll the chilled dough to a scant 1/4 inch (4-5mm) thick.
3. Using a 2.5 inch (6cm) round cutter, stamp out rounds and place them on the lined sheets. Re-roll the scraps once and cut more — you want an even number of rounds (about 20) so every biscuit gets a top and bottom.
4. Bake for 12-14 minutes, until the biscuits are set and just barely golden at the edges (they should stay pale, not browned). Let them firm up on the tray for 5 minutes, then transfer to a wire rack to cool completely — they will crisp as they cool.
5. Once fully cool, turn half the biscuits flat-side up and spread each with a generous teaspoon of raspberry jam, leaving a small clear border. Top each with a second biscuit, flat-side down, and press gently to sandwich. Keep the smoothest biscuits for the tops.
6. Make the water icing: sift the icing sugar into a bowl and stir in the water a teaspoon at a time until you have a thick but spreadable, glossy icing that slowly drips off the spoon. It should coat the back of a spoon without running off too fast.
7. Spoon icing onto the top of each sandwiched biscuit and spread it almost to the edge with the back of the spoon, letting it settle into a smooth surface. Work in small batches so the icing doesn't set before you decorate.
8. While the icing is still wet, press a glacé cherry half (or a jelly tot) into the center of each. Leave the biscuits undisturbed for at least 1 hour until the icing sets firm to the touch before serving or stacking.

</details>

#### 🍰 [Cassata Siciliana](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake2-cassata-siciliana/)
Sicilian · snack · hard  
**$1.42/serving** · 365 kcal · P9 · C47 · F16 · ⏱ 360 min · 🍽 12  

The crown jewel of Sicilian dolci: a moist pan di Spagna sponge baked from scratch, soaked in Marsala, layered around a sweet ricotta-and-dark-chocolate filling, wrapped in a band of almond marzipan, glazed with a thin lemon icing, and crowned with candied fruit and pistachios. Rich, festive, and unmistakably Sicilian.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Eggs** — 6 large eggs, separated (for the pan di Spagna sponge)
- **Sugar** — 3/4 cup granulated sugar (for the sponge)
- **All-purpose flour** — 1 1/4 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
- **Vanilla extract** — 1 tsp vanilla extract
- **Lemon zest** — 1 tsp grated lemon zest
- **Ricotta cheese** — 2 cups (about 1 lb) whole-milk ricotta, well drained overnight
- **Powdered sugar** — 3/4 cup powdered sugar (for the ricotta filling)
- **Dark chocolate** — 3 oz dark chocolate, finely chopped into small chips
- **Cinnamon** — 1/4 tsp ground cinnamon (for the ricotta)
- **Almond flour** — 1 1/2 cups almond flour (for the marzipan band)
- **Powdered sugar** — 1 cup powdered sugar (for the marzipan)
- **Almond extract** — 1/2 tsp almond extract (for the marzipan)
- **Marsala wine** — 6 tbsp sweet Marsala wine (for soaking the sponge), plus a splash for the marzipan
- **Powdered sugar** — 1 cup powdered sugar (for the lemon glacé icing)
- **Bottled lemon juice** — about 2 tbsp lemon juice (to thin the icing)
- **Dried apricots** — 1/2 cup mixed candied/dried fruit (dried apricots, plus citrus peel if available), for decorating
- **Pistachios** — 1 oz shelled pistachios, for garnish
- **Orange marmalade** — 2 tbsp orange marmalade, warmed, for glazing the candied fruit

**Steps**

1. Bake the pan di Spagna: Preheat the oven to 350F (175C) and butter and line a 9-inch round cake pan. Beat the 6 egg yolks with half the granulated sugar (about 6 tbsp) until pale and tripled in volume, then beat in the vanilla and lemon zest. In a clean bowl, whip the egg whites to soft peaks, gradually add the remaining sugar, and whip to firm, glossy peaks. Fold a third of the whites into the yolks to loosen, then gently fold in the rest alternately with the sifted flour, working quickly to keep the air. Pour into the pan and bake 28-32 minutes until golden and springy. Cool completely, then turn out.
2. Make the ricotta filling: Press the drained ricotta through a fine sieve into a bowl until completely smooth and creamy. Beat in the 3/4 cup powdered sugar and the cinnamon, then fold in the finely chopped dark chocolate. Cover and chill while you assemble everything else; it should be thick and spreadable.
3. Make the marzipan: Pulse or knead together the almond flour, 1 cup powdered sugar, almond extract, and a teaspoon or two of Marsala until it forms a smooth, pliable paste. (Tradition tints it pale green; leave it natural if you have no coloring.) Roll between two sheets of parchment into a long strip the height of your cake and long enough to line the inside of the pan.
4. Line the mold: Lightly oil the cleaned 9-inch pan and line it with plastic wrap. Slice the cooled sponge horizontally into 1/2-inch layers. Press the marzipan strip around the inside wall of the pan to form a collar, then trim sponge pieces to tile the bottom snugly. Brush the bottom sponge generously with Marsala.
5. Fill and top: Spread all of the ricotta filling over the bottom sponge layer inside the marzipan collar, leveling the top. Cover with a final layer of sponge cut to fit, brush that sponge with the remaining Marsala, and press gently so everything is compact. Cover and refrigerate at least 4 hours, ideally overnight, so the cake sets firm enough to unmold cleanly.
6. Unmold and glaze: Invert the chilled cassata onto a serving plate and peel away the plastic wrap; the marzipan band is now on the outside. Whisk the 1 cup powdered sugar with just enough lemon juice to make a thick but pourable glacé icing. Pour it over the top and let it flow to the marzipan edge, smoothing with an offset spatula so the icing coats the top in a clean white sheet.
7. Decorate: Once the icing has begun to set, arrange the candied/dried fruit in the traditional radial pattern over the top, scatter the pistachios, and brush the fruit with warmed marmalade for a glossy finish. Chill 30 minutes to set the decoration, then slice with a hot, dry knife and serve cold.

</details>

#### 🍮 [Malva Pudding](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake2-malva-pudding/)
South African · snack · easy  
**$0.57/serving** · 323 kcal · P4 · C45 · F14 · ⏱ 60 min · 🍽 8  

The classic South African baked sponge pudding: a soft, spongy caramel-toned cake spiked with apricot jam and a touch of vinegar, then drenched while warm in a hot butter-cream-sugar sauce that soaks right in. Sticky, tender and deeply comforting, traditionally served with custard or vanilla ice cream.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Sugar** — 3/4 cup white sugar (for the batter)
- **Eggs** — 2 large eggs
- **Apricot jam** — 2 tbsp apricot jam
- **All-purpose flour** — 1 cup all-purpose flour
- **Baking soda** — 1 tsp baking soda
- **Salt** — 1/4 tsp salt
- **Butter** — 1 tbsp butter, melted (for the batter)
- **Apple cider vinegar** — 1 tsp vinegar (cider or white)
- **Milk** — 1/2 cup whole milk
- **Heavy cream** — 1/2 cup heavy cream (for the sauce)
- **Butter** — 1/2 cup butter (for the sauce)
- **Sugar** — 1/2 cup sugar (for the sauce)
- **Water** — 1/4 cup hot water (for the sauce)
- **Vanilla extract** — 1 tsp vanilla extract (for the sauce)

**Steps**

1. Preheat the oven to 350F (180C) and butter a roughly 8-inch / 2-quart baking dish. Bring all batter ingredients to room temperature.
2. Make the batter base: beat the 3/4 cup sugar with the 2 eggs and 2 tbsp apricot jam on medium-high for about 3 minutes, until pale, thick and fluffy. This aeration is what gives malva its tender crumb.
3. In a small bowl, whisk together the flour, baking soda and salt. In a separate cup, stir the 1 tbsp melted butter and 1 tsp vinegar into the 1/2 cup milk.
4. Add the dry ingredients and the milk mixture to the egg mixture in alternating batches, mixing on low just until smooth. Do not overmix. The batter will be loose and pourable.
5. Pour the batter into the prepared dish and bake for 30-35 minutes, until risen, deep golden brown, and a skewer inserted in the center comes out clean. It should spring back when lightly pressed.
6. While the pudding bakes, make the sauce: combine the cream, 1/2 cup butter, 1/2 cup sugar and hot water in a saucepan. Heat gently, stirring, until the butter melts and the sugar dissolves, then bring to a simmer for 1 minute. Off the heat, stir in the vanilla.
7. As soon as the pudding comes out of the oven, pierce it all over with a skewer and slowly pour the hot sauce evenly over the hot pudding. It will look like too much liquid, but the sponge drinks it all up.
8. Let it stand 10-15 minutes so the sauce fully soaks in. Serve warm, scooped into bowls, with custard or vanilla ice cream.

</details>

#### 🧈 [Buttermilk Biscuits](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake-buttermilk-biscuits/)
Southern American · breakfast · medium  
**$0.37/serving** · 276 kcal · P4 · C32 · F14 · ⏱ 45 min · 🍽 8  

Tall, flaky Southern-style buttermilk biscuits with crisp golden tops and soft, steamy layers inside. Cold butter and tangy buttermilk plus a gentle hand are the whole secret — split and slather with butter, honey, or jam.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **All-purpose flour** — 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
- **Baking powder** — 1 tbsp baking powder
- **Baking soda** — 1/4 tsp baking soda
- **White sugar** — 1 tbsp sugar
- **Kosher salt** — 1 tsp
- **Butter** — 10 tbsp cold unsalted butter, plus 1 tbsp melted for brushing
- **Buttermilk** — 1 cup cold buttermilk, plus a splash if needed
- **Honey** — for serving _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. Heat oven to 450F. Whisk flour, baking powder, baking soda, sugar, and salt in a large bowl, then chill the bowl while you cut the butter.
2. Cube 10 tbsp of cold butter and toss it into the flour. Cut it in with your fingers or a pastry cutter until the largest pieces are the size of peas — visible butter flecks are what make the layers.
3. Make a well, pour in the cold buttermilk, and stir with a fork just until a shaggy, barely-cohesive dough forms. Add a splash more buttermilk only if there is dry flour left. Do not overmix.
4. Turn the dough onto a floured surface, pat into a 1-inch-thick rectangle, then fold it in thirds like a letter. Pat out and fold two more times — this builds the flaky layers.
5. Pat the dough to about 1 inch thick. Cut straight down with a floured 2.5-inch round cutter (do not twist, or the edges seal and won't rise). Press scraps together gently and cut the rest.
6. Place biscuits on a parchment-lined sheet with sides touching for tall, soft sides. Chill 10 minutes so the butter firms up again.
7. Brush the tops with melted butter and bake 15-18 minutes until deep golden brown and risen tall.
8. Cool 5 minutes, then split and serve warm with butter, honey, or jam.

</details>

#### 🥧 [Chess Pie](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake-chess-pie/)
Southern American · snack · easy  
**$0.70/serving** · 296 kcal · P4 · C40 · F14 · ⏱ 75 min · 🍽 8  

A Southern classic: a buttery, custardy pie with a thin sugary crackle on top. Egg yolks, plenty of sugar, melted butter, and a touch of cornmeal and vinegar set into a silky filling that's sweet but balanced by a little tang. Nothing fancy in the pantry, just the right technique.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Refrigerated pizza dough** — single 9-inch pie crust, store-bought or homemade pastry shell
- **Eggs** — 4 egg
- **Sugar** — about 1.5 cups granulated
- **Butter** — 1/2 cup (1 stick), melted and cooled
- **Cornmeal (polenta)** — 1 tablespoon, the defining chess-pie thickener
- **All-purpose flour** — 1 tablespoon
- **Vinegar** — 1 tablespoon white or apple cider vinegar for tang
- **Vanilla extract** — 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- **Milk** — 1/4 cup whole milk or buttermilk
- **Salt** — 1/4 teaspoon
- **Cornmeal (polenta)** — pinch extra to dust crust before filling _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Fit the pie crust into a 9-inch pie pan, crimp the edges, and chill it in the fridge while you make the filling so it holds its shape.
2. In a large bowl, whisk the sugar, cornmeal, flour, and salt together until evenly combined, breaking up any lumps.
3. Add the eggs and whisk just until smooth and uniform, then whisk in the cooled melted butter, milk, vinegar, and vanilla. Do not overbeat, you want it combined but not foamy.
4. Let the filling rest 5 minutes so the cornmeal hydrates, then skim off any surface foam for a cleaner-setting custard.
5. Pour the filling into the chilled crust. If the crust edges are browning fast, tent them with foil partway through baking.
6. Bake on the center rack for 45 to 55 minutes, until the edges are set and puffed and the center jiggles only slightly like soft Jell-O. A thin golden crackle should form on top.
7. Cool the pie completely on a rack, at least 2 to 3 hours, so the custard finishes setting before slicing.
8. Serve at room temperature or chilled, plain or with a dollop of lightly whipped cream.

</details>

#### 🧀 [Classic Southern Cheese Straws](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake2-cheese-straws/)
Southern American · snack · easy  
**$0.65/serving** · 137 kcal · P2 · C12 · F9 · ⏱ 65 min · 🍽 10  

Crisp, sharp, buttery cheese straws — the real Southern cocktail-party staple. Aged sharp cheddar is creamed with butter, then worked into a short flour dough seasoned with cayenne, pressed through a star tip (or rolled and cut), and baked until snappy. All the snap and tang of a good cheese cracker, with a clean cheddar finish and a slow build of heat.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Sharp cheddar block** — 8 oz extra-sharp cheddar (aged orange cheddar), finely grated, at room temperature
- **Butter** — 1/2 cup (1 stick) unsalted butter, softened
- **All-purpose flour** — 1 1/4 cups all-purpose flour
- **Salt** — 3/4 tsp salt
- **Cayenne pepper** — 1/2 tsp cayenne pepper (scant 1/4 tsp for mild, up to 3/4 tsp for spicy)
- **Paprika** — 1/4 tsp paprika, for color
- **Black pepper** — 1/4 tsp freshly ground black pepper

**Steps**

1. Let the butter and finely grated cheddar come fully to room temperature, about 30 minutes — cold cheese will not cream smoothly and the dough will crack. Use a block of extra-sharp cheddar grated on the small holes of a box grater; pre-shredded bagged cheese has anti-caking starch that makes the straws dry and crumbly.
2. In a stand mixer with the paddle (or a hand mixer), beat the softened butter and grated cheddar on medium speed for 2 to 3 minutes until the mixture is fluffy, evenly combined, and slightly lightened in color.
3. Whisk together the flour, salt, cayenne, paprika, and black pepper in a separate bowl. Add to the cheese mixture and beat on low just until a cohesive dough forms and pulls away from the sides — stop the moment it comes together. If it looks dry and sandy, beat 30 seconds more; the warmth of mixing brings it together. Do not add water.
4. Press the dough into a disk, wrap, and chill 20 to 30 minutes so it firms up enough to pipe or roll cleanly. Meanwhile, heat the oven to 350F (175C) and line two baking sheets with parchment.
5. To pipe: load the dough into a cookie press or a piping bag fitted with a large open-star tip and press out ribbons across the sheet, then cut into roughly 3-inch lengths. To roll and cut: roll the dough 1/8 inch thick on a lightly floured surface and cut into 3-inch by 1/2-inch strips, or run through a hand-crank press. Space the straws about 1/2 inch apart.
6. Bake 13 to 16 minutes, rotating the pans halfway, until the straws are firm, set, and just barely golden at the edges. They should not brown much — pull them while still pale or they turn bitter. The centers crisp fully as they cool.
7. Cool on the pans for 5 minutes, then transfer to a rack to cool completely; warm straws are fragile and soft, and only reach full snap once cold. Store airtight at room temperature up to 1 week, or freeze the raw piped dough and bake straight from frozen, adding a minute or two.

</details>

#### 🍰 [Classic Southern Lane Cake](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake2-lane-cake/)
Southern American · snack · hard  
**$1.35/serving** · 531 kcal · P7 · C80 · F20 · ⏱ 150 min · 🍽 16  

The real-deal Lane Cake from 1898 Alabama: four tender layers of white butter cake lightened with stiff-beaten egg whites, sandwiched with a rich bourbon-laced egg-yolk custard packed with raisins, toasted pecans, and coconut, then cloaked in fluffy seven-minute boiled white frosting. A celebration cake worth the effort.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **All-purpose flour** — 3 1/4 cups all-purpose flour (sifted), for the cake layers
- **Baking powder** — 3 1/2 tsp baking powder
- **Salt** — 1/2 tsp salt
- **Butter** — 1 cup (2 sticks) unsalted butter, softened, for the cake
- **Sugar** — 2 cups granulated sugar, for the cake
- **Whole milk** — 1 cup whole milk, room temperature
- **Vanilla extract** — 1 tsp vanilla extract, for the cake
- **Eggs** — 8 large egg whites for the cake (reserve the 8 yolks for the filling)
- **Sugar** — 1 cup granulated sugar, for the bourbon custard filling
- **Butter** — 1/2 cup (1 stick) unsalted butter, for the filling
- **Bourbon** — 1/4 cup (2 oz) bourbon whiskey, for the filling
- **Raisins** — 1 cup raisins, finely chopped
- **Pecans** — 1 cup (about 4 oz) pecans, toasted and chopped
- **Coconut flakes** — 1 cup sweetened shredded/flaked coconut
- **Vanilla extract** — 1 tsp vanilla extract, for the filling
- **Eggs** — 2 large egg whites for the seven-minute frosting
- **Sugar** — 1 1/2 cups granulated sugar, for the frosting
- **Water** — 1/3 cup cold water, for the frosting
- **Corn syrup** — 1 tbsp light corn syrup, for the frosting
- **Cream of tartar** — 1/4 tsp cream of tartar, for the frosting
- **Vanilla extract** — 1 tsp vanilla extract, for the frosting

**Steps**

1. Preheat oven to 350F. Butter four 9-inch round cake pans and line the bottoms with parchment (or bake in two batches with two pans). Sift together the 3 1/4 cups flour, baking powder, and salt; set aside. Separate 8 eggs, reserving the yolks in a bowl for the filling.
2. Make the cake batter: Cream the 1 cup softened butter with 2 cups sugar until very pale and fluffy, 4-5 minutes. Add the milk and flour mixture alternately in three additions, beginning and ending with flour and mixing just until smooth; beat in 1 tsp vanilla. In a separate clean bowl, whip the 8 egg whites to stiff peaks, then gently fold them into the batter in three additions to keep it airy.
3. Divide the batter evenly among the prepared pans and smooth the tops. Bake 18-22 minutes, until the layers spring back and a pick comes out clean. Cool in the pans 10 minutes, then turn out onto racks, peel off the parchment, and cool completely.
4. Make the bourbon custard filling: In a heavy saucepan off the heat, whisk the 8 reserved egg yolks with 1 cup sugar until smooth, then add the 1/2 cup butter and 1/4 cup bourbon. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly, until the mixture thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon and reads about 170F, 8-12 minutes; do not let it boil or the yolks will scramble.
5. Off the heat, stir the chopped raisins, toasted pecans, coconut, and 1 tsp vanilla into the hot custard. Let it cool to room temperature, where it will thicken into a spreadable, jammy filling.
6. Stack the cake: Place the first layer on a plate or stand and spread a third of the filling to the edges; repeat with the second and third layers. Top with the fourth layer. Leave the top and sides bare for now so they're ready for frosting.
7. Make the seven-minute frosting: In a heatproof bowl set over (not touching) simmering water, combine the 2 egg whites, 1 1/2 cups sugar, 1/3 cup water, 1 tbsp corn syrup, and 1/4 tsp cream of tartar. Beat constantly with a hand mixer on high until the frosting holds stiff, glossy peaks, about 7 minutes. Remove from the heat and beat in 1 tsp vanilla.
8. Working quickly while the frosting is fluffy, swirl it generously over the top and sides of the cake, pulling up decorative peaks. Let the cake stand at least a few hours (ideally overnight, loosely covered) so the boozy filling mellows and the layers soften before slicing.

</details>

#### 🍰 [Hummingbird Cake](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake-hummingbird-cake/)
Southern American · snack · medium  
**$2.18/serving** · 653 kcal · P8 · C68 · F41 · ⏱ 90 min · 🍽 12  

A classic Southern layer cake — tender, spiced crumb packed with mashed banana, crushed pineapple, and toasted pecans, all crowned with a tangy cream cheese frosting. No mixer required; everything comes together with a whisk and a bowl.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **All-purpose flour** — all-purpose flour, ~3 cups
- **Sugar** — ~2 cups granulated sugar
- **Baking soda** — 1 tsp
- **Cinnamon** — 1 tsp ground
- **Salt** — 1/2 tsp
- **Eggs** — 3 egg
- **Vegetable oil** — ~1 cup neutral oil
- **Vanilla extract** — 1.5 tsp extract
- **Banana** — very ripe, mashed (~1.75 cups)
- **Pineapple** — 1 can crushed pineapple, undrained (~8 oz)
- **Pecans** — ~1.5 cups chopped, divided for batter and garnish
- **Cream cheese** — 16 oz, softened, for frosting
- **Butter** — 1/2 cup softened, for frosting
- **Powdered sugar** — ~4 cups for frosting

**Steps**

1. Heat oven to 350F (175C). Grease and line two 9-inch round cake pans with parchment. Spread the pecans on a dry sheet and toast 6-8 minutes until fragrant, then cool and reserve about a third for garnish.
2. In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, granulated sugar, baking soda, cinnamon, and salt until evenly combined.
3. In a second bowl, whisk the eggs, oil, and vanilla until smooth, then stir in the mashed banana and the undrained crushed pineapple.
4. Pour the wet mixture into the dry ingredients and fold gently with a spatula just until no dry streaks remain — do not overmix. Fold in the toasted pecans for the batter.
5. Divide the batter evenly between the two pans and bake 25-30 minutes, until a toothpick in the center comes out clean and the tops spring back. Cool in the pans 10 minutes, then turn out onto a rack to cool completely.
6. Make the frosting: beat the softened cream cheese and butter until smooth, then add the powdered sugar a little at a time and beat until light and fluffy. Chill 10 minutes if it feels soft.
7. Level the cooled cakes if domed. Place one layer on a plate, spread with frosting, then top with the second layer and frost the top and sides.
8. Press the reserved toasted pecans around the top edge, slice, and serve. Store leftovers covered in the fridge.

</details>

#### 🍰 [Southern Caramel Cake](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake2-caramel-cake/)
Southern American · snack · hard  
**$1.02/serving** · 608 kcal · P5 · C94 · F24 · ⏱ 110 min · 🍽 12  

A tender, fine-crumbed Southern butter layer cake blanketed in the real thing: a cooked-on-the-stove caramel icing made by caramelizing brown sugar with butter and milk, then beating it to a glossy fudge-like frosting. This is the heirloom cake you'd find on a glass cake stand at a church supper — buttery, sweet, and unmistakably caramel, with that signature slightly grainy, set icing that crackles a little under the fork.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **All-purpose flour** — 3 cups cake flour (or all-purpose), sifted
- **Baking powder** — 2 1/2 tsp baking powder
- **Salt** — 1/2 tsp salt
- **Butter** — 1 cup (2 sticks) unsalted butter, softened — for the cake
- **Sugar** — 2 cups granulated sugar
- **Eggs** — 4 large eggs, room temperature
- **Vanilla extract** — 2 tsp vanilla extract
- **Whole milk** — 1 cup whole milk, room temperature
- **Brown sugar** — 2 cups packed light brown sugar — for the caramel icing
- **Butter** — 1/2 cup (1 stick) unsalted butter — for the icing
- **Whole milk** — 1/2 cup whole milk (or evaporated milk) — for the icing
- **Vanilla extract** — 1 tsp vanilla extract — for the icing
- **Salt** — 1/4 tsp salt — for the icing
- **Powdered sugar** — 2 cups powdered sugar, sifted — to finish the icing

**Steps**

1. Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease two 9-inch round cake pans, line the bottoms with parchment, and grease again. Whisk together the 3 cups flour, baking powder, and 1/2 tsp salt in a bowl and set aside.
2. Make the batter: Beat the 1 cup softened butter and 2 cups granulated sugar on medium-high for 4-5 minutes until pale and fluffy. Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each, then beat in the 2 tsp vanilla. Scrape the bowl down.
3. On low speed, add the flour mixture in three additions alternating with the 1 cup milk (begin and end with flour), mixing just until each addition disappears. Do not overmix. Divide evenly between the two pans and smooth the tops.
4. Bake 26-32 minutes, until the tops spring back and a toothpick comes out clean. Cool in the pans 10 minutes, then turn out onto a rack, peel off the parchment, and cool completely before icing.
5. Make the caramel icing: In a heavy 3-quart saucepan, combine the 2 cups brown sugar, 1/2 cup butter, 1/2 cup milk, and 1/4 tsp salt. Cook over medium heat, stirring, until the butter melts and the sugar dissolves, then bring to a rolling boil.
6. Clip on a candy thermometer and boil, stirring occasionally, until it reaches the soft-ball stage, 235-238°F, about 5-7 minutes. Remove from the heat and stir in the 1 tsp vanilla.
7. Let the caramel cool to about 200°F, then add the 2 cups sifted powdered sugar and beat vigorously with a wooden spoon (or hand mixer) for 2-4 minutes until smooth, glossy, and just thick enough to spread — it will keep firming up, so work promptly.
8. Assemble quickly: place one layer on a plate, spread a generous layer of warm icing on top, set the second layer over it, and pour/spread the rest over the top and down the sides, working fast before it sets. Let the cake stand at least 30 minutes so the icing firms into its signature fudgy finish before slicing.

</details>

#### 🧁 [Magdalenas (Classic Spanish Lemon Madeleines)](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake2-magdalenas/)
Spanish · snack · easy  
**$0.44/serving** · 209 kcal · P3 · C26 · F11 · ⏱ 95 min · 🍽 12  

Traditional Spanish magdalenas — tall, domed little cakes with the signature cracked peak, a crisp sugary top, and a tender lemon-and-olive-oil crumb. The batter is whipped until pale and thick, rested cold so it rises into a proud belly, and baked hot so the tops split. Nothing like a French madeleine: these are fluffy, fragrant, and made for dunking in coffee or hot chocolate.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Eggs** — 3 large eggs, at room temperature
- **Sugar** — 3/4 cup granulated sugar, plus extra for sprinkling tops
- **Olive oil** — 1/2 cup mild extra-virgin olive oil (or light olive oil)
- **Milk** — 1/4 cup whole milk, room temperature
- **All-purpose flour** — 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
- **Baking powder** — 2 tsp baking powder
- **Lemon zest** — finely grated zest of 1 lemon (about 2 tsp)
- **Salt** — 1/4 tsp salt
- **Vanilla extract** — 1 tsp vanilla extract
- **Cinnamon** — 1/4 tsp ground cinnamon (optional, traditional) _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. In a large bowl, beat the eggs and 3/4 cup sugar with electric beaters on high for 5-8 minutes, until very pale, thick, and tripled in volume. This long whip is what gives magdalenas their height — don't rush it. The mixture should fall in a ribbon that holds on the surface for a few seconds.
2. With the beaters on low, drizzle in the olive oil in a slow steady stream, then the milk, vanilla, and lemon zest, mixing just until combined. Keep the mixing gentle from here on so you don't deflate the eggs.
3. Sift together the flour, baking powder, salt, and optional cinnamon. Add to the bowl in two additions, folding with a spatula until just incorporated and no dry streaks remain. The batter will be thick and airy.
4. Cover the bowl and refrigerate for at least 1 hour (up to overnight). Chilling firms the batter and is the secret to a tall, cracked dome — skipping it gives flat magdalenas.
5. Place paper magdalena liners (or muffin liners) into a 12-cup muffin tin; the rigid tin helps them rise up rather than out. Preheat the oven to 425F (220C) with a rack in the center.
6. Fill each liner about three-quarters full with the cold batter. Sprinkle a generous pinch of granulated sugar over each top — this creates the classic crackly, sugary crust.
7. Bake at 425F for 5 minutes, then without opening the oven reduce to 375F (190C) and bake another 10-13 minutes, until deeply golden, domed, and split on top. A toothpick should come out clean.
8. Cool in the tin for 5 minutes, then transfer to a rack. Serve slightly warm or at room temperature — perfect dunked in coffee, milk, or hot chocolate. Store airtight up to 4 days.

</details>

#### 🍮 [Quesada Pasiega (Cantabrian Baked Curd Cake)](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake2-quesada-pasiega/)
Spanish · snack · easy  
**$0.75/serving** · 196 kcal · P4 · C24 · F9 · ⏱ 75 min · 🍽 9  

The real, classic dessert from the Pas Valley in Cantabria, northern Spain — a dense, golden baked curd cake somewhere between a custard and a cheesecake. Fresh curd cheese is enriched with browned butter, eggs, sugar, a little flour, milk, lemon zest and cinnamon, then baked hot until the top cracks and caramelizes. Tender and faintly tangy inside, with a deep toasted-butter aroma. Best made a day ahead and served at room temperature in thin squares.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Ricotta cheese** — 1 cup (about 250 g) fresh ricotta, the closest stand-in for traditional Pasiega curd cheese (cuajada/requesón) — drain if very wet
- **Cottage cheese** — 1/2 cup (about 125 g) full-fat cottage cheese, blended smooth, to mimic the loose fresh-curd texture
- **Eggs** — 3 large eggs
- **Sugar** — 3/4 cup (150 g) granulated sugar
- **Butter** — 6 tbsp (85 g) unsalted butter, browned and cooled slightly
- **All-purpose flour** — 1/2 cup (65 g) all-purpose flour
- **Whole milk** — 1/2 cup (120 ml) whole milk
- **Lemon zest** — Finely grated zest of 1 lemon (about 2 tsp)
- **Cinnamon** — 1 tsp ground cinnamon, plus a little extra for dusting
- **Salt** — 1/4 tsp fine salt
- **Vanilla extract** — 1/2 tsp vanilla extract (optional, rounds out the flavor) _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. Heat the oven to 220C / 425F. Generously butter an 8-inch (20 cm) square baking dish or a similar ceramic dish and lightly dust with flour, tapping out the excess. The high starting heat is what gives a real quesada its cracked, burnished top.
2. Brown the butter: melt the 6 tbsp butter in a small pan over medium heat, swirling, until it foams, the milk solids turn golden-brown, and it smells nutty (3-4 minutes). Pour into a bowl and let cool for 5 minutes — this toasted-butter note is signature to the dish.
3. In a large bowl, blend the ricotta and cottage cheese until as smooth as possible (use a blender, food processor, or push through a sieve). Traditional cuajada is loose and slightly grainy, but a smoother base bakes more evenly.
4. Whisk in the eggs one at a time, then the sugar, salt, lemon zest, cinnamon and vanilla until fully combined and the sugar starts to dissolve.
5. Whisk in the milk, then the cooled browned butter. Sift in the flour and whisk just until you have a smooth, pourable batter with no lumps — do not overbeat. The batter should be thin, like a loose pancake batter.
6. Pour into the prepared dish (the batter should sit about 3 cm / just over an inch deep). Dust the surface lightly with extra cinnamon. Bake at 220C / 425F for 10 minutes to set and color the top.
7. Lower the oven to 180C / 350F and bake another 30-35 minutes, until the top is deep golden-brown and cracked, the edges are set, and the center has only a slight wobble. A skewer inserted in the middle should come out with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.
8. Turn off the oven, crack the door, and let the quesada cool inside for 15 minutes to keep it from sinking, then cool completely on a rack. Refrigerate several hours (ideally overnight) — it firms up and the flavor deepens. Serve at room temperature, cut into squares.

</details>

#### 🌀 [Ensaïmada de Mallorca](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake2-ensa-mada/)
Spanish (Mallorcan) · breakfast · hard  
**$0.79/serving** · 552 kcal · P10 · C67 · F26 · ⏱ 90 min · 🍽 6  

The classic Mallorcan breakfast pastry: a slow-proofed enriched dough stretched paper-thin, smeared with pork lard, rolled into a rope, coiled into its iconic spiral, proofed overnight, baked until golden, and snowed with powdered sugar. Airy, faintly chewy, and shatteringly delicate — exactly the version you'd find in a Palma bakery.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **All-purpose flour** — 3 1/2 cups bread flour (strong flour), plus extra for dusting
- **Sugar** — about 1/3 cup plus 2 tbsp granulated sugar (7 tbsp)
- **Eggs** — 2 large eggs, room temperature
- **Water** — 1/2 cup lukewarm water
- **Instant yeast** — 2 tsp instant yeast
- **Olive oil** — 2 tbsp mild olive oil (helps the dough relax for stretching)
- **Salt** — 1 tsp fine salt
- **Butter** — about 2/3 cup softened pork lard for spreading — substitute softened butter (10 tbsp) if lard is unavailable
- **Powdered sugar** — 1/4 cup powdered sugar, for dusting (4 tbsp)

**Steps**

1. Make the dough: In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, granulated sugar, yeast, and salt. Make a well and add the eggs, lukewarm water, and olive oil. Mix to a shaggy mass, then knead on a clean surface for 12-15 minutes until very smooth, elastic, and glossy. This is a stiff-ish enriched dough; resist adding extra flour. Form into a ball.
2. First rest: Lightly oil the dough, cover, and let it rest at warm room temperature for about 1 hour, until relaxed and slightly puffed. It will not double yet — this rest is mainly to make the dough extensible for stretching.
3. Divide and pre-shape: Punch down gently and divide into 4 equal pieces (for 4 small ensaïmadas) or keep whole for one large one. Roll each piece into a tight ball, cover, and rest 15 minutes so the gluten relaxes.
4. Stretch and grease: On a generously oiled work surface, press and stretch one ball with oiled hands into a thin rectangle, then keep stretching gently from underneath until almost translucent — large and paper-thin, like strudel dough. Smear the entire surface evenly with softened lard (or butter), right to the edges.
5. Roll and coil: Starting from a long edge, roll the dough up into a long thin rope, sealing as you go. Gently stretch the rope longer, then wind it into a loose flat spiral on a parchment-lined tray, leaving small gaps so it has room to expand. Tuck the end underneath.
6. Overnight proof: Cover loosely and proof in a warm spot for 6-12 hours (overnight is traditional), until visibly puffy, airy, and nearly doubled. A long slow proof is what gives the ensaïmada its characteristic light, layered crumb.
7. Bake: Heat the oven to 350F (175C). Lightly mist or brush the coils with water. Bake 18-22 minutes for small ones (25-30 for one large), until deep golden and the spirals are set. If browning too fast, tent with foil.
8. Finish: Cool on a rack for at least 15 minutes, then dust thickly and evenly with powdered sugar through a fine sieve just before serving. Best eaten the same day, ideally still slightly warm, with coffee.

</details>

#### 🍰 [Swiss Roll](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake-swiss-roll/)
Swiss · snack · medium  
**$0.52/serving** · 141 kcal · P4 · C26 · F3 · ⏱ 55 min · 🍽 8  

A featherlight whisked sponge baked thin, rolled warm so it keeps its curl, then unrolled, spread with raspberry jam, and re-rolled into a tender spiral dusted with powdered sugar. The classic teatime jam roll done with real genoise technique.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Eggs** — large, at room temperature
- **White sugar** — about 100g for the sponge
- **All-purpose flour** — about 90g all-purpose, sifted
- **Vanilla extract** — 1 tsp
- **Salt** — pinch
- **Jam** — seedless raspberry, about 2/3 cup
- **Powdered sugar** — for rolling and dusting

**Steps**

1. Heat the oven to 425F (220C). Line a 10x15-inch rimmed sheet pan with parchment, leaving a slight overhang, and lightly grease it.
2. In a large bowl set over a pan of simmering water, whisk the eggs and white sugar until just warm to the touch, then take off the heat and beat with a mixer on high for 5-7 minutes until pale, tripled in volume, and thick enough to leave a ribbon trail.
3. Beat in the vanilla. Sift the flour and salt over the eggs in two additions, folding gently with a spatula each time until just combined so you keep the air in the batter.
4. Pour into the prepared pan and spread to an even layer, nudging it into the corners. Bake 7-9 minutes, until the sponge is golden and springs back when lightly pressed.
5. While it bakes, lay a clean kitchen towel flat and dust it generously with powdered sugar. Invert the hot sponge onto the towel, peel off the parchment, and trim any crisp edges.
6. Starting from a short end, roll the warm sponge up snugly with the towel inside and let it cool completely rolled up, about 30 minutes. This sets the curl so it won't crack.
7. Gently unroll the cooled sponge, spread the raspberry jam evenly almost to the edges, then re-roll tightly without the towel.
8. Set the roll seam-side down, dust with more powdered sugar, and slice with a serrated knife to serve.

#### 🍍 [Taiwanese Pineapple Cake (鳳梨酥)](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake2-taiwanese-pineapple-cake/)
Taiwanese · snack · medium  
**$0.36/serving** · 123 kcal · P2 · C14 · F7 · ⏱ 120 min · 🍽 16  

Authentic Taiwanese pineapple shortcakes: buttery, crumbly milk-powder-rich pastry pressed around a sticky, slow-cooked pineapple jam filling and baked in little square molds. The classic gift-box treat, made the way a careful home baker would — real cooked-down pineapple, a tender melt-in-the-mouth crust, and just enough salt to balance the sweetness.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Butter** — 1/2 cup (1 stick) unsalted butter, softened — for the pastry
- **Powdered sugar** — 1/4 cup powdered sugar — for the pastry
- **Salt** — 1/4 tsp salt
- **Eggs** — 1 large egg, room temperature
- **All-purpose flour** — 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour (sift before measuring for a finer crumb)
- **Milk** — about 1/4 cup whole milk powder — closest catalog match for the classic milk-powder flavor in the crust
- **Cornstarch** — 2 tbsp cornstarch (for a more tender, sandy texture)
- **Pineapple** — 4 cups crushed fresh or canned pineapple, well-drained (about two 20-oz cans), for the jam filling
- **Sugar** — about 1/3 cup (5 tbsp) granulated sugar for the jam — adjust to the tartness of your pineapple
- **Butter** — 1 tbsp butter, to finish and gloss the cooked jam
- **Bottled lemon juice** — 1 tbsp lemon juice, to brighten the filling _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. Make the jam first (it needs to cool): squeeze the drained crushed pineapple firmly to remove as much juice as possible, then add the pulp to a wide nonstick or heavy skillet. Reserve the juice. Cook over medium heat, stirring often, until most of the moisture evaporates, about 15-20 minutes.
2. Stir the sugar (and lemon juice, if using) into the pineapple. Lower the heat to medium-low and keep cooking, stirring constantly as it thickens, 15-25 minutes more, until the jam is deep golden, glossy, and holds its shape when you drag a spatula across the pan. Stir in 1 tbsp butter at the end, then spread on a plate to cool completely. You should have a stiff, sticky paste — it firms up further as it cools.
3. Make the dough: beat the softened butter, powdered sugar, and salt with a wooden spoon or mixer until smooth and pale, 2-3 minutes. Beat in the egg until fully combined and creamy.
4. Sift together the flour, milk powder, and cornstarch, then fold into the butter mixture in two additions just until a soft dough forms. Do not overwork it. Press into a disc, wrap, and chill 30 minutes while the oven heats.
5. Heat the oven to 340 F (170 C). Divide the cooled jam into 16 equal pieces (about 15 g each) and roll each into a ball. Divide the dough into 16 pieces (about 20 g each).
6. Flatten a piece of dough in your palm, set a ball of jam in the center, and gently wrap the dough up and around the filling, pinching to fully seal so no jam shows. Roll into a smooth ball.
7. Set each ball into a square pineapple-cake mold (or a greased mini square/muffin tin) on a parchment-lined sheet, pressing gently to fill the corners and flatten the top. If using rings, leave them on for baking to keep the crisp square edge.
8. Bake 12 minutes, then carefully flip each cake (in its mold) and bake 10-13 minutes more, until the edges and bottoms are pale golden — they should not brown deeply. The pastry stays light in color.
9. Let the cakes cool in the molds 10 minutes, then unmold and cool completely on a rack. They firm up and the flavor deepens as they rest; store airtight for up to a week.

</details>

#### 🍪 [Şekerpare (Turkish Semolina Cookies in Lemon Syrup)](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake2-ekerpare/)
Turkish · snack · medium  
**$0.33/serving** · 240 kcal · P2 · C37 · F10 · ⏱ 110 min · 🍽 18  

Şekerpare are soft, melt-in-the-mouth Turkish semolina-and-flour cookies, each crowned with a blanched almond, baked until golden and then drowned in a cool lemon-scented sugar syrup. The contrast of the warm, just-baked cookie soaking up cold syrup is what makes them irresistible — they turn dense, moist, and almost custardy inside while staying lightly crisp on top. A teahouse classic served all over Turkey.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Butter** — 3/4 cup (170g) unsalted butter, melted and cooled
- **Powdered sugar** — 1/2 cup powdered (icing) sugar
- **Eggs** — 1 large egg, plus 1 yolk (reserve white for brushing if desired)
- **Vegetable oil** — 2 tbsp neutral vegetable oil
- **Vanilla extract** — 1 tsp vanilla extract
- **Semolina** — 1/2 cup fine semolina
- **All-purpose flour** — 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour (add gradually until a soft, non-sticky dough forms)
- **Baking powder** — 1 1/2 tsp baking powder
- **Lemon zest** — 1 tsp finely grated lemon zest
- **Almonds** — about 18 blanched almonds (1/4 cup), one per cookie
- **Sugar** — 2 cups granulated sugar (for the syrup)
- **Water** — 1 1/2 cups water (for the syrup)
- **Bottled lemon juice** — 1 tbsp lemon juice (or juice of half a fresh lemon)

**Steps**

1. Make the syrup first so it has time to cool completely: combine the granulated sugar and water in a saucepan, bring to a boil, then lower the heat and simmer gently for 12-15 minutes until slightly thickened. Stir in the lemon juice and simmer 1 minute more. Remove from heat and let cool fully to room temperature — cold syrup over warm cookies is the secret to the right texture.
2. Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C) and line a baking tray with parchment. In a large bowl, whisk the melted butter, oil, and powdered sugar until smooth, then beat in the whole egg, the extra yolk, vanilla, and lemon zest.
3. Whisk together the semolina, baking powder, and most of the flour, then add to the wet mixture. Knead gently, adding the remaining flour a little at a time, until you get a soft dough that just stops sticking to your hands — do not overwork it or the cookies turn hard.
4. Pinch off walnut-sized pieces (about 1 1/2 tbsp each) and roll into smooth balls. Place on the tray about 1 1/2 inches apart, flatten the tops very slightly, and press one blanched almond firmly into the center of each. For extra shine, brush the tops with a little reserved egg white.
5. Bake for 20-25 minutes, until the tops are pale golden and the bottoms are lightly browned. They should be set but not dark — they continue to firm up as they soak.
6. As soon as the cookies come out of the oven, ladle the cooled syrup evenly over the hot cookies right on the tray (or transfer to a shallow dish first). You will hear them sizzle. Use all the syrup.
7. Let the cookies sit and absorb the syrup for at least 1 hour, spooning any pooled syrup back over them once or twice, until the syrup is fully soaked in and the cookies are plump and glossy.
8. Serve at room temperature with Turkish tea. They keep covered at room temperature for 3-4 days and only get better as they rest.

</details>

#### 🍞 [Paska (Ukrainian Easter Bread)](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/bake2-paska-bread/)
Ukrainian · breakfast · medium  
**$0.53/serving** · 316 kcal · P7 · C50 · F10 · ⏱ 230 min · 🍽 12  

A tall, golden, lightly sweet enriched yeast bread from Ukraine, traditionally baked for Easter. The crumb is soft, buttery, and tender from a rich dough of eggs, milk, butter, and sugar, perfumed with vanilla and orange zest. A glossy egg wash gives the classic burnished top. Best baked in a tall round tin (a clean coffee can or panettone mold works) and served in thick slices, plain or with butter.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **All-purpose flour** — 4 1/2 cups all-purpose flour (plus a little for kneading)
- **Whole milk** — 1 cup whole milk, warmed to about 110F
- **Active dry yeast** — 1 packet (2 1/4 tsp) active dry yeast
- **Sugar** — 3/4 cup granulated sugar
- **Butter** — 1/2 cup unsalted butter, melted and slightly cooled
- **Eggs** — 4 large eggs (3 for the dough, 1 reserved for the egg wash)
- **Salt** — 1 tsp salt
- **Vanilla extract** — 2 tsp vanilla extract
- **Lemon zest** — 1 tsp orange zest (use a lemon's worth from one orange) _(optional)_
- **Raisins** — 1/4 cup golden raisins, soaked and drained _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. Proof the yeast: in a small bowl combine the warm whole milk (about 110F), 1 tablespoon of the sugar, and the packet of active dry yeast. Stir once and let stand 8-10 minutes until foamy. If it does not foam, your milk was too hot or the yeast is dead start over.
2. Build the wet base: in a large bowl whisk 3 of the eggs with the remaining sugar, the salt, vanilla, and orange zest until pale. Whisk in the melted (cooled) butter, then the foamy yeast-milk mixture.
3. Make the dough: add the flour about one cup at a time, stirring with a wooden spoon until a shaggy, sticky dough forms. Turn out onto a floured surface and knead 8-10 minutes (or 6-7 minutes in a stand mixer with the dough hook) until smooth, elastic, and only slightly tacky add flour sparingly so the bread stays tender. If using raisins, knead them in at the end.
4. First rise: place the dough in a greased bowl, cover, and let rise in a warm spot 1 1/2 to 2 hours until doubled. This rich dough rises slowly be patient.
5. Shape: grease a tall round tin (a clean large coffee can, panettone mold, or 8-inch round cake pan with a parchment collar). Punch down the dough, reserve a small fist-sized piece if you want to make a traditional braided cross, and shape the rest into a smooth ball. Set it in the tin it should fill it no more than halfway.
6. Second rise: cover loosely and let rise again 45-60 minutes until the dough crowns just above the rim. Meanwhile, arrange any reserved dough into a braid or cross on top, and heat the oven to 350F (175C).
7. Egg wash and bake: beat the reserved egg with a teaspoon of water and gently brush the entire top. Bake at 350F for 40-50 minutes until deep golden and the internal temperature reads about 190F. If the top browns too fast, tent loosely with foil after 25 minutes.
8. Cool and serve: let cool in the tin 10 minutes, then turn out onto a rack and cool fully before slicing this lets the tender crumb set. Slice into thick rounds; lovely plain, with butter, or lightly toasted.
