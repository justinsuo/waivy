- **Olive oil** — 1 tbsp

#### 🍚 [Rice Cooker Cilantro Lime Rice](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/rice-cooker-cilantro-lime-rice/)
Mexican-inspired · meal-prep · easy  
**$0.35/serving** · 378 kcal · P7 · C78 · F4 · ⏱ 35 min · 🍽 4  

Fluffy Chipotle-style cilantro-lime rice that tastes like a burrito bowl base, made hands-free in your rice cooker.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Jasmine rice** — long-grain white rice, Chipotle-style
- **Lime** — 1 for cooking, 1 for finishing
- **Fresh cilantro** — about 1/2 bunch, finely chopped
- **Vegetable oil** — 1 tbsp, stirred in for fluff
- **Salt** — about 1 tsp, to taste
- **Bay leaves** — 1 leaf for subtle aroma, Chipotle-style _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. Rinse 1 cup (dry) jasmine rice per 2 servings under cold water until the water runs mostly clear, then drain and add to the rice cooker pot with 2 cups of water.
2. Stir in 1 tablespoon vegetable oil, 1 teaspoon salt, the juice of 1 lime, and the optional bay leaf so the rice cooks seasoned all the way through.
3. Close the lid and run a normal White Rice / Cook cycle until it clicks to Warm, then let it rest on Warm for 10 minutes without opening so the steam finishes the grains.
4. Remove and discard the bay leaf, then fluff the rice gently with a fork or rice paddle to separate the grains.
5. Fold in the chopped cilantro and the juice of the second lime while the rice is still warm so the flavor soaks in.
6. Taste and add a pinch more salt or a squeeze of lime if needed, then serve as a base for burrito bowls, tacos, or grilled proteins.

</details>

#### 🧆 [Blender Hummus](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/blender-hummus/)
Middle Eastern · snack · easy  
**$0.68/serving** · 163 kcal · P5 · C17 · F9 · ⏱ 5 min · 🍽 4  

Ultra-smooth, lemony chickpea hummus that comes together in the blender in under five minutes. Tahini, garlic, and a splash of cold water whip it into a creamy dip for pita, veggies, or sandwiches.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Chickpeas (canned)** — one 15 oz can, drained and rinsed (reserve a little liquid)
- **Tahini** — 1/4 cup, well stirred
- **Lemon** — juiced, about 3 tbsp
- **Garlic** — 1 small clove
- **Olive oil** — tbsp, plus more to drizzle
- **Cumin** — 1/2 tsp ground
- **Salt** — 1/2 tsp, to taste
- **Water** — tbsp ice water, added gradually
- **Paprika** — pinch, for garnish _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. Drain and rinse the chickpeas. For the smoothest texture, pinch off and discard any loose skins (optional but worth it).
2. Add tahini, lemon juice, garlic, cumin, and salt to the blender. Blend 20-30 seconds until pasty and lightened.
3. Add the chickpeas and olive oil. Blend, scraping down the sides as needed, until thick and grainy.
4. With the blender running, stream in ice water 1 tablespoon at a time until silky and smooth, about 1-2 minutes total. Taste and adjust salt and lemon.
5. Scrape into a bowl, swirl with a spoon, drizzle with olive oil, and dust with paprika before serving.

</details>

#### 🍲 [Moroccan Lamb Tagine](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/cur2-moroccan-lamb-tagine/)
Moroccan · dinner · medium  
**$3.94/serving** · 66 kcal · P2 · C14 · F1 · ⏱ 150 min · 🍽 4  

A soul-warming Moroccan braise of meltingly tender lamb shoulder slow-cooked with ras el hanout, ginger, saffron and sweet dried apricots, brightened by preserved lemon and finished with toasted almonds and cilantro. Deeply spiced but balanced, with a glossy, fragrant sauce meant for spooning over couscous.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Lamb shoulder** — boneless, cut into 1.5-inch chunks
- **Onion** — 1 large, finely grated/chopped
- **Garlic** — 4 cloves, minced
- **Fresh ginger** — 1 tbsp fresh grated
- **Ras el hanout** — 2 tbsp
- **Cumin** — 1 tsp ground
- **Coriander (ground)** — 1 tsp ground
- **Cinnamon** — 1/2 tsp, plus 1 stick if available
- **Turmeric** — 1/2 tsp
- **Saffron threads** — large pinch, bloomed in warm water
- **Crushed tomatoes** — about half a can
- **Chicken broth** — ~2 cups, or lamb/beef stock
- **Dried apricots** — about 1 cup, halved
- **Honey** — 1 tbsp
- **Preserved lemon** — rind of 1, rinsed and slivered _(optional)_
- **Olive oil** — 2 tbsp for searing
- **Slivered almonds** — 1/3 cup, toasted, for garnish
- **Fresh cilantro** — 1/4 cup chopped, plus parsley
- **Salt** — to taste
- **Black pepper** — to taste

**Steps**

1. Pat the lamb chunks dry and season generously with salt and pepper. Bloom the saffron in 3 tbsp warm water and set aside. In a small bowl, mix the ras el hanout, cumin, coriander, cinnamon and turmeric.
2. Heat the olive oil in a heavy Dutch oven or tagine over medium-high. Sear the lamb in batches until deeply browned on all sides, about 8 minutes total; transfer to a plate. Do not crowd the pot, or the meat will steam instead of brown.
3. Lower the heat to medium and add the grated onion. Cook 6-8 minutes until softened and golden, scraping up the browned bits. Stir in the garlic and ginger and cook 1 minute until fragrant.
4. Add the spice mix and cook 30-60 seconds, stirring constantly, until it smells toasty. Stir in the crushed tomato and cook 2-3 minutes to deepen the flavor.
5. Return the lamb and its juices to the pot. Pour in the chicken broth and bloomed saffron with its water, plus a cinnamon stick if using. Bring to a gentle simmer, then cover and reduce heat to low.
6. Braise gently for about 1.5 hours, stirring occasionally, until the lamb is fork-tender. Add a splash of water if it ever looks dry.
7. Stir in the dried apricots, honey and preserved lemon. Uncover and simmer 20-30 more minutes, until the apricots plump and the sauce reduces to a glossy, spoonable consistency. Taste and adjust salt.
8. Meanwhile, toast the slivered almonds in a dry pan over medium heat until golden, 3-4 minutes; watch closely so they don't burn.
9. Rest the tagine 5 minutes off the heat. Scatter with toasted almonds and chopped cilantro, and serve over couscous or with warm bread.

</details>

#### 🍳 [Shakshuka with Feta](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/cur2-shakshuka-with-feta/)
North African / Middle Eastern · breakfast · easy  
**$2.12/serving** · 254 kcal · P13 · C16 · F16 · ⏱ 40 min · 🍽 4  

Eggs gently poached in a spiced tomato and roasted-pepper sauce, finished with crumbled feta and fresh herbs. A bold North African and Middle Eastern classic that comes together in one skillet — serve straight from the pan with crusty bread for scooping.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Olive oil** — 3 tbsp
- **Onion** — 1 large, finely diced
- **Bell pepper** — 1 red bell pepper, diced
- **Garlic** — 4 cloves, minced
- **Cumin** — 2 tsp ground cumin
- **Sweet paprika** — 2 tsp
- **Smoked paprika** — 1 tsp
- **Cayenne pepper** — 1/4 tsp, or to taste
- **Tomato paste** — 2 tbsp
- **Crushed tomatoes** — 1 28-oz can
- **Salt** — to taste
- **Black pepper** — to taste
- **Sugar** — 1 tsp, to balance acidity
- **Eggs** — 6 large eggs
- **Feta cheese** — about 4 oz, crumbled
- **Fresh cilantro** — handful, chopped — or parsley _(optional)_
- **Bread** — crusty bread, for serving

**Steps**

1. Heat the olive oil in a large, deep skillet over medium heat. Add the diced onion and red bell pepper and cook, stirring occasionally, for 8-10 minutes until softened and the onion is golden at the edges.
2. Stir in the minced garlic, cumin, sweet paprika, smoked paprika, and cayenne. Cook for 1 minute until fragrant, then add the tomato paste and fry it for another minute to deepen its flavor.
3. Pour in the crushed tomatoes and add the sugar, a good pinch of salt, and a few grinds of pepper. Simmer uncovered, stirring now and then, for 10-15 minutes until the sauce thickens and the raw tomato edge cooks off. Taste and adjust seasoning.
4. Crumble about half the feta into the sauce and stir it in. Using the back of a spoon, make 6 shallow wells in the sauce, spacing them evenly.
5. Crack one egg into each well. Spoon a little sauce over the whites only, leaving the yolks exposed, and season the eggs with a pinch of salt.
6. Cover the skillet and reduce the heat to low. Cook for 8-12 minutes, until the whites are just set but the yolks are still runny — check often, as they go from runny to firm quickly.
7. Scatter the remaining crumbled feta and the chopped cilantro or parsley over the top. Serve immediately from the pan with crusty bread for scooping.

</details>

#### 🍛 [Kadai Chicken](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/cur2-kadai-chicken/)
North Indian · dinner · medium  
**$3.84/serving** · 372 kcal · P27 · C14 · F23 · ⏱ 50 min · 🍽 4  

A North Indian dhaba classic cooked karahi-style: bone-in chicken simmered in a thick, clinging masala of freshly pounded coriander seeds and dried red chiles, with onions, tomatoes, and capsicum kept just-tender for bite. Finished with garam masala, crushed kasoori methi, and julienned ginger for a fragrant, semi-dry curry that hugs every piece.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Chicken thighs** — bone-in, skinless preferred, cut into curry pieces
- **Ground coriander** — 2 tbsp, dry-roasted and coarsely crushed — the signature kadai masala
- **Dried chili** — 4 whole dried red chiles (e.g. Kashmiri), pounded with the coriander
- **Onion** — 1 finely chopped for the base, 1 cut into petals to toss in at the end
- **Tomato** — ripe, finely chopped or pureed
- **Bell pepper** — capsicum, cut into 1-inch squares
- **Ginger paste** — 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
- **Garlic** — 6 cloves, crushed
- **Ghee** — 3 tbsp (or mustard/veg oil)
- **Cumin seeds** — 1 tsp for tempering
- **Turmeric** — 1/2 tsp
- **Chili powder** — 1 tsp Kashmiri red chili powder, for color
- **Garam masala** — 1 tsp, added near the end
- **Kasoori methi (dried fenugreek)** — 1 tbsp dried fenugreek leaves, crushed between palms
- **Fresh ginger** — a 1-inch knob, julienned for garnish
- **Fresh cilantro** — small handful, chopped, to finish
- **Salt** — to taste
- **Plain whole-milk yogurt** — 2 tbsp, whisked — optional for a slightly richer gravy _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. Dry-roast the coriander seeds and whole dried red chiles in a hot pan for 1-2 minutes until fragrant, then pound coarsely in a mortar (or pulse) into a chunky kadai masala. Set aside.
2. Heat the ghee in a kadai or heavy wok over medium-high heat. Add the cumin seeds and let them crackle, then add the crushed garlic and finely chopped onion. Cook 5-6 minutes until the onion is soft and golden at the edges.
3. Stir in the ginger-garlic paste and cook 1 minute, then add the turmeric, Kashmiri chili powder, and about two-thirds of the pounded kadai masala; fry 30 seconds until it smells toasty.
4. Add the chopped tomatoes and salt. Cook down 6-8 minutes, mashing, until the tomatoes break into a thick pulpy masala and the ghee starts to separate at the sides. (Stir in the optional whisked yogurt now for a richer gravy.)
5. Add the chicken pieces and turn to coat in the masala. Sear 3-4 minutes, then cover and cook 12-15 minutes over medium heat, stirring occasionally, until the chicken is cooked through and the masala is thick and clinging. Add a splash of water only if it sticks.
6. Raise the heat, add the capsicum squares and onion petals, and toss for 3-4 minutes so they soften slightly but keep their crunch and the gravy reduces to a semi-dry coat.
7. Sprinkle in the garam masala and the remaining kadai masala, crush the kasoori methi between your palms over the pan, and stir through for 1 minute.
8. Taste for salt and chili, finish with julienned ginger and chopped cilantro, and serve hot with naan or jeera rice.

</details>

#### 🍢 [Chicken Kebab Koobideh](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/cur2-chicken-kebab-koobideh/)
Persian · dinner · medium  
**$1.77/serving** · 711 kcal · P14 · C152 · F4 · ⏱ 60 min · 🍽 4  

Persian-style ground chicken kebab — minced chicken kneaded with grated onion, turmeric, and saffron until tacky, then molded onto skewers (or shaped into logs) and grilled over high heat for a juicy, smoky char. Served with saffron rice, grilled tomato, and sumac.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Ground chicken** — use thigh-based ground chicken if available — higher fat keeps it juicy
- **Onion** — large yellow onion, grated and squeezed of excess juice
- **Turmeric** — 1 tsp
- **Saffron threads** — pinch, bloomed in 2 tbsp hot water
- **Salt** — 1.5 tsp
- **Black pepper** — 1/2 tsp, freshly ground
- **Sumac** — for dusting at the table
- **Butter** — 2 tbsp, melted for basting
- **Tomato** — grilled alongside the kebabs
- **Basmati rice** — steamed, for serving
- **egg-yolk** — mapped to eggs — optional binder for leaner chicken _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. Bloom the saffron: crush a pinch of saffron threads and steep in 2 tbsp very hot water; set aside to deepen in color. Grate the onion on the large holes of a box grater, then transfer to a fine sieve and press out as much liquid as you can — wet onion will make the mince fall apart on the skewer.
2. In a large bowl combine the ground chicken, squeezed grated onion, turmeric, salt, pepper, and half the saffron water (add the optional egg yolk if your chicken is very lean). Knead vigorously with your hands for 5-8 minutes until the mixture turns smooth, pale, and noticeably tacky — this protein binding is what lets it grip the skewer.
3. Cover and chill the mince for at least 30 minutes (up to overnight). Cold, rested mince is firmer and far easier to shape.
4. With wet hands, divide into 4-6 portions. Press each firmly along a flat metal skewer in a long even log, pinching ridges every inch with your fingers; or shape into flattened logs directly on a foil-lined tray if you have no skewers.
5. Heat a grill, grill pan, or broiler to high. Halve the tomatoes and thread or place them alongside. Grill the kebabs without moving them for 3-4 minutes until the underside chars and releases cleanly, then flip.
6. Baste with melted butter and the remaining saffron water, and grill the second side 3-4 minutes more until just cooked through (165F / no pink) but still juicy — chicken koobideh cooks fast, so don't overdo it.
7. Rest the kebabs 2 minutes off the heat. Serve over steamed basmati rice with the blistered grilled tomatoes, and dust generously with sumac at the table.

</details>

Sweet, sour, and nutty all at once, it's a centerpiece dish traditionally served over fluffy Persian rice for celebrations and Yalda night.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Chicken thighs** — bone-in, skin removed (or 8 thighs for a crowd)
- **Walnuts** — about 2.5 cups, finely ground and toasted
- **Pomegranate molasses** — about 2/3 cup; the defining souring agent
- **Onion** — grated or finely diced
- **Butter** — 2 tbsp, for sauteing the onion
- **Sugar** — 2 tbsp, to balance tartness; adjust to taste
- **Turmeric** — 1/2 tsp
- **Cinnamon** — 1/4 tsp ground
- **Nutmeg** — pinch, optional warming spice _(optional)_
- **Saffron threads** — small pinch bloomed in hot water _(optional)_
- **Chicken broth** — about 2 cups to thin the walnut sauce
- **Salt** — 0.5 tsp
- **Black pepper** — 0.25 tsp
- **Pomegranate arils** — for garnish _(optional)_
- **Basmati rice** — steamed, to serve

**Steps**

1. Toast the walnuts: in a dry skillet over medium-low heat, toast the ground walnuts, stirring constantly, for 4-6 minutes until fragrant and lightly golden but not browned. This deepens flavor and releases their oils. Set aside.
2. In a heavy pot or Dutch oven, melt the butter over medium heat and saute the grated onion with the turmeric until soft and pale gold, about 8 minutes. Season the chicken thighs with salt and pepper and brown them in the pot for 2-3 minutes per side, then set the chicken aside.
3. Add the toasted ground walnuts to the pot and stir for 1-2 minutes to coat in the onion and butter, then pour in the chicken broth plus 1 cup water. Bring to a gentle simmer.
4. Simmer the walnut sauce uncovered on low for 30-40 minutes, stirring often so it does not catch, until the walnut oil rises and the sauce darkens and thickens. Skim off any oil if you like a leaner sauce.
5. Stir in the pomegranate molasses, sugar, cinnamon, nutmeg, and bloomed saffron. Taste and balance: it should be boldly tart with a gentle sweet backbone. Add more molasses for sour or sugar for sweet as needed.
6. Return the chicken (and any juices) to the pot, nestling the pieces into the sauce. Cover and simmer gently on low for 45-60 minutes, turning the chicken once, until it is fork-tender and the sauce is glossy and deep mahogany brown.
7. Uncover for the last 10 minutes if the sauce needs thickening; it should coat the back of a spoon. Adjust salt.
8. Rest 10 minutes off the heat to let the flavors marry and the oil settle. Garnish with fresh pomegranate seeds and serve hot over steamed basmati rice.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Salt cod** — bacalhau, soaked 24-36h with water changes to desalt

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Jasmine rice** — steamed, for serving
- **Pork chop** — boneless, pounded thin
- **Char siu (BBQ pork)** — store-bought or leftover, sliced _(optional)_
- **Cabbage** — green cabbage, roughly chopped for braising
- **Eggs** — fried, one per serving
- **Curry powder** — Malaysian/Singapore-style meat curry powder, tablespoons
- **Coconut milk** — for the gravy
- **Potato** — cubed, simmered in the curry
- **Onion** — 1 onion
- **Garlic** — cloves
- **Fresh ginger** — thumb, minced
- **Shallot** — 2 shallot
- **Chicken broth** — for the curry base
- **Cornstarch** — tablespoons, for dredging pork (split with flour)
- **All-purpose flour** — for the pork chop batter/dredge
- **White pepper** — teaspoons, for marinade and gravy

6. Fry the eggs in a little of the same oil, keeping the yolks runny with crisp lacy edges.
7. Assemble each plate: mound the steamed jasmine rice, lay over the sliced pork chop, braised cabbage, and char siu, then top with a fried egg.

#### 🍲 [Idli Sambar](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/cur2-idli-sambar/)

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Rice** — idli rice or parboiled rice, soaked for the batter
- **Urad dal** — skinned split black gram for the idli batter
- **Fenugreek seed** — fenugreek seeds, soaked with the urad dal for fermentation
- **Toor dal** — split pigeon peas, the base of the sambar
- **Tamarind paste** — soak a walnut-size piece or use 2 tsp concentrate
- **Turmeric** — 0.1 tsp
- **Shallot** — small sambar onions or pearl onions, halved
- **Tomato** — 1 tomato
- **Carrot** — 1 carrot
- **Okra** — or use drumstick (moringa) pieces if available
- **Garam masala** — use sambar powder if you have it
- **Coriander (ground)** — ground coriander, backbone of sambar powder
- **Dried chili** — dried red chilies for heat and the tempering
- **Mustard seeds** — black mustard seeds for the tadka
- **Curry leaves** — fresh curry leaves, essential for aroma
- **Ghee** — or coconut oil for the tempering
- **Fresh cilantro** — fresh coriander leaves to finish
- **Salt** — 0.1 tsp
- **Cumin** — a pinch of hing/asafoetida is traditional here if you have it _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. Make the batter (start a day ahead): rinse the rice and soak it separately from the urad dal and fenugreek seeds for 4-6 hours. 2. Steam the idli: lightly grease idli molds, pour in the fermented batter to fill each cup three-quarters, and steam over simmering water for 10-12 minutes until a toothpick comes out clean. Let rest 2 minutes before unmolding so they release cleanly.
3. Cook the dal: rinse the toor dal and pressure-cook (or simmer) with turmeric and water until completely soft and creamy, about 15 minutes in a pressure cooker. Mash smooth and set aside.
4. Soak the tamarind paste in 1 cup warm water, then strain to get a tangy extract (skip the straining if using smooth concentrate).
5. Build the sambar: in a pot, simmer the chopped carrot, okra, tomato, and halved shallots with the tamarind extract, ground coriander, garam masala/sambar powder, a broken dried chili, and salt until the vegetables are tender, 8-10 minutes.
6. Stir the mashed dal into the vegetables, add water to a pourable, soupy consistency, and simmer gently 5-7 minutes so the flavors meld. Taste and balance salt and tang.
7. Make the tempering (tadka): heat the ghee in a small pan until shimmering, add the mustard seeds and let them pop, then add the remaining dried chili and the curry leaves and fry a few seconds until fragrant.
8. Pour the sizzling tempering over the sambar and stir it through. Finish with chopped fresh coriander.
9. Serve the hot idli alongside generous bowls of sambar, ladling extra sambar over the idli so they soak it up.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **All-purpose flour** — all-purpose: ~1 1/4 cups for the crust plus 3 tbsp to thicken the filling
- **Butter** — 1/2 cup cold cubed for crust; 6 tbsp melted for filling
- **Buttermilk** — 1 cup full-fat, well shaken

8. Plate each steak and ladle the hot peppery gravy generously over the top. Serve immediately while the crust is still crisp.

#### 🍞 [Southern Cornbread Dressing](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/cur2-cornbread-dressing/)
Southern American · dinner · medium  
**$0.62/serving** · 72 kcal · P4 · C6 · F4 · ⏱ 110 min · 🍽 8  

The real Thanksgiving-table version: a skillet of buttermilk cornbread baked, crumbled, and mixed with day-old bread, the holy trinity of onion-celery-bell pepper sweated in butter, lots of sage and poultry seasoning, and enough chicken broth to make it spoonably moist. Bound with egg and baked until the edges go crisp and golden while the inside stays custardy. This is dressing, baked in a dish — not stuffing crammed in a bird.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Cornmeal (polenta)** — yellow cornmeal for the cornbread base (about 2 cups)
- **All-purpose flour** — all-purpose, about 1 cup, for the cornbread
- **Buttermilk** — about 1.5 cups for the cornbread batter
- **Eggs** — 1 in the cornbread batter, 3 to bind the dressing
- **Baking powder** — 1 tbsp for the cornbread
- **Baking soda** — 1/2 tsp for the cornbread
- **Bread** — 3-4 slices day-old white bread or 2 leftover biscuits, torn
- **Butter** — 1 stick: part for the skillet, rest to sweat the vegetables
- **Onion** — yellow onion, finely diced
- **Celery** — about 3 ribs, finely diced
- **Bell pepper** — optional but traditional in much of the South
- **Chicken broth** — about 4-5 cups; add until the mix is wet and loose
- **Dried sage** — rubbed sage, the defining flavor — about 1 tbsp
- **Poultry seasoning** — about 2 tsp
- **thyme-dried** — 0.1
- **Salt** — 0.1 tsp
- **Black pepper** — lots of black pepper

**Steps**

1. Make the cornbread first, ideally a day ahead so it dries out. Heat oven to 425F. Put 2 tbsp of the butter in a cast-iron skillet or 8-inch pan and slide it into the oven to melt and get hot. Whisk together the cornmeal, flour, baking powder, baking soda, and a big pinch of salt; in another bowl whisk the buttermilk with 1 egg, then stir the wet into the dry until just combined.
2. Swirl the hot butter to coat the pan, pour in the batter, and bake 20-22 minutes until golden and set. Cool, then crumble the whole cornbread into a very large bowl along with the torn day-old bread. Leave it uncovered for a few hours or overnight to stale — dressing made with fresh, soft bread turns to mush.
3. Lower the oven to 350F and butter a 9x13 baking dish. Melt the remaining butter in a skillet over medium heat and add the onion, celery, and bell pepper with a pinch of salt. Sweat 8-10 minutes until completely soft and translucent but not browned — this is the backbone of the flavor, so don't rush it.
4. Scrape the vegetables and all their butter into the bowl of crumbled bread. Add the sage, poultry seasoning, thyme, 1 tsp salt, and plenty of black pepper, and toss until the bread is evenly coated. Taste a pinch of the seasoned crumbs — it should taste a touch too salty and sage-forward, because the broth will mellow it.
5. Pour in about 4 cups of the warm chicken broth and the 3 remaining beaten eggs, then fold gently. The mixture should be very wet and loose, almost soupy — like thick oatmeal. Add more broth as needed; under-moistened dressing bakes out dry, so err wet.
6. Spread the dressing into the buttered dish without packing it down. Dot the top with a little extra butter if you have it. Bake uncovered at 350F for 40-50 minutes, until the top is golden brown and crisp and the center is set but still moist when you press it.
7. Let it rest 10 minutes before serving so it firms up enough to scoop. Serve hot, ideally with turkey or chicken and a ladle of gravy over the top.

</details>

#### 🍅 [Gazpacho Andaluz](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/cur2-gazpacho-andaluz/)

The classic chilled raw-tomato soup of Andalusia: ripe tomatoes, cucumber, green pepper and garlic blended silky-smooth with day-old bread, sherry vinegar and a generous stream of olive oil. No cooking, no fuss, just deeply refreshing on a hot day.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Tomato** — very ripe Roma or vine tomatoes, the soul of the dish
- **Cucumber** — peeled
- **Bell pepper** — green Italian/Cubanelle pepper preferred
- **Garlic** — 1 small clove, raw
- **Bread** — about 2 thick slices stale rustic bread, crusts removed
- **Sherry vinegar** — 2 tbsp
- **Extra virgin olive oil** — good Spanish extra-virgin, drizzled in to emulsify

**Steps**

7. Chill in the fridge for at least 2 hours, ideally longer, until very cold.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

The Spanish tapas bar classic: a thick, silky béchamel folded with finely diced jamón serrano, chilled until firm, then breaded and fried into crisp golden croquettes with a molten, creamy center. Patience while chilling the masa is the real secret.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Sliced ham** — jamón serrano, finely diced (cured Spanish ham)
- **Whole milk** — about 2.5 cups, warmed
- **Butter** — about 5 tbsp
- **All-purpose flour** — about 3/4 cup
- **Onion** — very finely minced _(optional)_
- **Nutmeg** — a pinch, freshly grated

**Steps**

7. Heat olive oil to 180C/350F in a deep pan. Fry the croquetas in small batches for 2-3 minutes, turning, until deeply golden all over — don't crowd the pan or the oil will cool.
8. Lift out with a slotted spoon and drain on paper towels. Rest 1-2 minutes and serve hot, while the centers are still molten and creamy.

#### 🥘 [Spanish Paella Valenciana](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/cur2-spanish-paella-valenciana/)

The original paella from the rice fields of Valencia — bone-in chicken and rabbit browned in olive oil, a slow tomato-and-pimentón sofrito, flat green beans and butter beans, then short-grain rice simmered in saffron-rosemary broth until it forms the prized toasted crust (socarrat) on the bottom. No seafood, no stirring, no cream.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Chicken thighs** — bone-in, cut into chunks
- **Chicken drumsticks** — traditionally part rabbit; use bone-in chicken pieces
- **Arborio rice** — bomba or other short-grain Spanish rice
- **Chicken broth** — about 4 cups hot broth
- **Green beans** — wide flat romano beans if available
- **White beans (canned)** — for traditional garrofó butter beans
- **Tomato** — grated, skins discarded
- **Olive oil** — 0.25 tbsp
- **Smoked paprika** — Spanish pimentón dulce
- **Saffron threads** — a generous pinch of threads
- **Fresh rosemary** — 1 sprig
- **Garlic** — 3 clove
- **Salt** — 1 tsp
- **Lemon** — wedges to serve _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. Bloom the saffron: warm the chicken broth and steep the saffron threads in it (and a little salt) so it turns deep gold while you cook.
2. Heat the olive oil in a wide paella pan or large skillet over medium-high. Season the bone-in chicken pieces with salt and brown them well on all sides, about 8-10 minutes, then push them to the outer ring of the pan.
3. Add the flat green beans and butter beans to the center and saute a couple of minutes until blistered.
4. Make the sofrito: lower the heat, add the grated garlic and grated tomato, and cook down slowly for 5-7 minutes until jammy and the oil separates, then stir in the smoked paprika off the direct heat so it doesn't scorch.

#### 🍳 [Spanish Tortilla Española](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/cur2-spanish-tortilla-espanola/)

The classic Spanish potato omelette: thin-sliced potatoes and onion gently confited in olive oil, bound with beaten eggs and cooked into a thick, golden cake that's set on the outside and just-creamy in the middle. Five ingredients, all technique. Serve in wedges warm or at room temperature.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Potato** — about 1.5 lb / 700g waxy or Yukon Gold potatoes
- **Eggs** — 6 egg
- **Onion** — yellow onion, thinly sliced (classic 'con cebolla')
- **Olive oil** — extra virgin, enough to nearly submerge the potatoes (most is drained and reusable)
- **Salt** — to taste

**Steps**

1. Peel the potatoes and slice them into thin, even rounds about 2-3mm thick (a mandoline helps). Thinly slice the onion. Pat the potatoes dry and salt them lightly.
2. Pour the olive oil into a 9-10 inch nonstick skillet over medium-low heat. Add the potatoes and onion, making sure they're nearly submerged. 4. Beat the eggs in a large bowl with a good pinch of salt. Fold in the warm drained potatoes and onion and let the mixture rest 10-15 minutes so the potatoes soak up the egg.

6. Cover the pan with a flat plate larger than the skillet. Holding it firmly, flip the tortilla onto the plate, then slide it back into the pan uncooked-side down. Tuck the edges in with a spatula to round the sides.

Silky salt-and-sugar-cured salmon buried in fresh dill, cured cold for two to three days and sliced paper-thin, served with a sweet mustard-dill sauce.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Salmon fillet** — 1 skin-on, center-cut, sushi-grade salmon fillet (~2 lb / 900 g), pin bones removed
- **Kosher salt** — about 1/3 cup coarse salt for the cure
- **White sugar** — about 1/4 cup, equal-ish to salt by feel
- **Fresh dill** — 2 large bunches, roughly chopped with stems
- **Whole black peppercorns** — 1 Tbsp white peppercorns, coarsely crushed
- **Vodka** — 2 Tbsp, optional rub to firm the surface _(optional)_
- **Dijon mustard** — for the hovmästarsås sauce
- **Honey** — or sugar, to sweeten the mustard sauce
- **White wine vinegar** — for the sauce
- **Vegetable oil** — neutral oil to emulsify the sauce
- **Sourdough bread** — rye or sourdough crispbread, to serve _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. Run your fingers along the salmon and pull any pin bones with tweezers. Pat the fillet completely dry and, if using, rub the flesh side with vodka to firm the surface and help the cure adhere.
2. Make the cure: combine the kosher salt, sugar, and coarsely crushed white peppercorns. Spread a thin layer of cure plus a handful of chopped dill on a sheet of plastic wrap large enough to wrap the whole fillet.
3. Lay the salmon skin-side down on the dill bed. Pack the remaining cure firmly all over the flesh, then bury the top completely under the rest of the chopped dill, pressing it in.

6. While it cures, whisk the hovmästarsås: Dijon, honey, and white wine vinegar, then slowly drizzle in the oil while whisking to a loose mayo-like emulsion. Stir in a tablespoon of finely chopped dill and chill.
7. Unwrap the cured salmon, scrape off the dill and cure, and pat dry. Do not rinse unless it tastes too salty (a quick rinse and re-dry is fine if so).
8. Set the fillet skin-side down and slice on a sharp shallow angle into thin, wide sheets, leaving the skin behind. Fan the slices on a board.
9. Serve cold with the mustard-dill sauce, extra fresh dill, and rye crispbread or sourdough. Keeps wrapped in the fridge up to 5 days.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

- **Fresh dill** — chopped, to finish

- **Black pepper** — 1 tsp
- **White beans (canned)** — optional; some regions add white beans _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. Put the bone-in beef in a large pot with about 3 quarts cold water, bring to a gentle boil, and skim off the gray foam. Add the bay leaves and a big pinch of salt, then lower to a bare simmer for 1.5 to 2 hours until the meat is tender. Lift out the meat, pull it from the bone into bite-size pieces, and reserve the strained broth.
2. Meanwhile make the zazharka: heat the oil in a skillet over medium, soften the diced onion and grated carrot for about 6 minutes, then stir in the tomato paste and cook 2 minutes until it darkens and smells sweet.
3. Add the grated beets to the skillet with the splash of vinegar and the pinch of sugar (the acid locks in the ruby color). Add a ladle of broth, cover, and braise gently for 15 to 20 minutes until the beets are tender but still vivid.
4. Bring the reserved broth back to a simmer, add the cubed potato, and cook 10 minutes until almost tender. Add the shredded cabbage (and white beans if using) and cook another 5 to 7 minutes.
5. Stir the beet zazharka and the shredded beef back into the pot. Simmer everything together for 10 minutes so the flavors marry; taste and adjust salt, pepper, and acidity.
6. Turn off the heat and stir in the crushed raw garlic. Cover and let the borscht rest at least 15 minutes (it is even better the next day).
7. Ladle into bowls, top each with a spoonful of sour cream and a generous handful of fresh dill, and serve with dark bread.

</details>

#### 🥞 [Vietnamese Banh Xeo (Sizzling Crepes)](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/cur2-vietnamese-banh-xeo/)
Vietnamese · dinner · medium  
**$4.53/serving** · 271 kcal · P27 · C8 · F16 · ⏱ 75 min · 🍽 4  

Crispy, lacy turmeric-coconut rice crepes that sizzle when the batter hits the hot pan — hence the name. Folded over shrimp, pork belly, and crunchy bean sprouts, then torn into lettuce with fresh herbs and dunked in nuoc cham. Crackly edges, savory filling, bright dipping sauce: the full Southern Vietnamese experience.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Rice flour** — 2 cups rice flour for the batter
- **Cornstarch** — 2 tbsp, for extra crisp edges
- **Turmeric** — 1 tsp ground turmeric for the signature golden color
- **Coconut milk** — about 1 cup; thin the rest with water
- **Water** — roughly 1.5 cups to reach a thin, pourable batter
- **Scallion** — green parts thinly sliced, stirred into batter
- **Salt** — 1 tsp, for batter
- **Pork belly** — about 1/2 lb, thinly sliced
- **Shrimp (frozen)** — peeled, deveined, halved lengthwise
- **Bean sprouts** — about 3 cups, a generous handful per crepe
- **Vegetable oil** — for frying the crepes
- **Lettuce** — soft leaf lettuce for wrapping
- **Fresh mint** — fresh leaves for the herb plate
- **Fresh cilantro** — fresh sprigs for the herb plate
- **Fish sauce** — for nuoc cham dipping sauce
- **Lime** — juiced for nuoc cham
- **Sugar** — 3 tbsp, for nuoc cham
- **Garlic** — 2 cloves minced for nuoc cham
- **Thai chili** — minced, to taste, for nuoc cham
- **Carrot** — julienned into the nuoc cham _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. Make the batter: whisk 2 cups rice flour, 2 tbsp cornstarch, 1 tsp turmeric, and 1 tsp salt, then whisk in 1 cup coconut milk and about 1.5 cups water until smooth and thin like heavy cream. Stir in the sliced scallion greens and let it rest at least 30 minutes (or overnight in the fridge) for the crispest crepes.
2. Make nuoc cham: stir 3 tbsp sugar into 6 tbsp warm water until dissolved, then add the lime juice, fish sauce, minced garlic, and Thai chili. Taste and balance sweet-sour-salty; toss in the julienned carrot. Set aside.
3. Prep the fillings: thinly slice the pork belly and season with a pinch of salt; halve the shrimp lengthwise. Wash and dry the lettuce, mint, and cilantro and arrange on a platter for wrapping.
4. Heat a nonstick or well-seasoned skillet over medium-high. Add a little oil, then a few slices of pork belly and a few shrimp pieces; cook until the pork renders and the shrimp turn pink, 2 to 3 minutes.
5. Stir the batter (it settles), then ladle a thin layer into the hot pan and immediately swirl to coat the bottom in a lacy round — it should sizzle loudly. Drizzle a little oil around the edges.
6. Scatter a generous handful of bean sprouts over one half, cover the pan, and steam 1 to 2 minutes to cook the sprouts and set the crepe.
7. Uncover and let the crepe fry until the edges turn deep golden, crisp, and lift away from the pan, another 2 to 3 minutes — patience here is what makes it crackly.
8. Fold the crepe in half over the filling and slide onto a plate. Repeat with the remaining batter, oiling the pan between crepes.
9. To eat, tear a piece of crepe, tuck it into a lettuce leaf with mint and cilantro, roll it up, and dip generously in nuoc cham. Serve immediately while crisp.

</details>

#### 🍜 [Vietnamese Bun Cha](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/cur2-vietnamese-bun-cha/)
Vietnamese · dinner · medium  
**$3.30/serving** · 46 kcal · P2 · C11 · F0 · ⏱ 55 min · 🍽 4  

Hanoi's iconic grilled pork dish: smoky caramelized pork belly and seasoned pork patties served in a warm sweet-and-sour fish sauce broth with pickled carrot and daikon, alongside a heap of cool rice vermicelli and a mountain of fresh herbs. You assemble each bite at the table by dipping noodles and herbs into the broth.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Ground pork** — for the seasoned pork patties (cha)
- **Pork belly** — thinly sliced, for the grilled pork strips
- **Rice vermicelli** — bun - dried rice vermicelli
- **Fish sauce** — split between marinade and dipping broth
- **Sugar** — for caramel notes in marinade and nuoc cham
- **Shallot** — minced, for marinade
- **Garlic** — minced, for marinade and dipping sauce
- **Lemongrass** — finely minced, optional but traditional _(optional)_
- **White pepper** — 0.05 tsp
- **Vegetable oil** — for grilling/pan
- **Lime** — fresh juice for the dipping broth
- **Rice vinegar** — for quick pickle and broth balance
- **Carrot** — julienned for the pickle
- **Daikon radish** — julienned for the pickle
- **Thai chili** — sliced, for the dipping broth _(optional)_
- **Lettuce** — soft leaf lettuce for the herb plate
- **Fresh mint** — fresh mint leaves
- **Fresh cilantro** — 0.5 tbsp
- **Thai basil** — 0.5 bag _(optional)_
- **Bean sprouts** — 0.3 cup _(optional)_

**Steps**

1. Make the marinade: combine minced shallot, half the garlic, the minced lemongrass, 2 tbsp fish sauce, 1 tbsp sugar, the white pepper, and 1 tbsp oil. Divide between two bowls. Mix the ground pork into one bowl and form into small flat patties; toss the thinly sliced pork belly in the other. Marinate both at least 30 minutes (or overnight).
2. Quick-pickle: toss the julienned carrot and daikon with the rice vinegar, 1 tbsp sugar, and a pinch of salt. Let sit at least 20 minutes, then squeeze lightly.
3. Make the nuoc cham dipping broth: dissolve 3 tbsp sugar in 1 cup warm water, then stir in 3 tbsp fish sauce and the lime juice. Taste and balance so it is sweet, sour, and savory but light enough to sip. Stir in the remaining minced garlic and sliced thai chili. Add a small handful of the pickled vegetables to each serving bowl.
4. Cook the rice vermicelli in boiling water until just tender, about 4-5 minutes, then drain and rinse under cold water. Loosen into loose nests and set aside at room temperature.
5. Grill the pork over high heat (charcoal, grill pan, or broiler) until deeply caramelized and lightly charred on the edges — about 3-4 minutes per side for the patties and 2-3 minutes for the belly slices. Char is essential for authentic smoky flavor.
6. Warm the dipping broth gently if it has cooled — it should be served warm, not hot. Drop the hot grilled pork patties and belly directly into the warm broth so the juices mingle.
7. Arrange the herb plate: lettuce, mint, cilantro, thai basil, and bean sprouts.
8. Serve family-style: each person gets a bowl of warm pork broth and a plate of vermicelli and herbs. To eat, dip noodles and herbs into the broth, grab some pork and pickles, and enjoy bite by bite.

</details>

#### 🥬 [Vietnamese Summer Rolls (Gỏi Cuốn)](https://justinsuo.github.io/waivy/recipes/cur2-summer-rolls/)
Vietnamese · lunch · medium  
**$3.71/serving** · 136 kcal · P26 · C8 · F1 · ⏱ 40 min · 🍽 4  

Fresh, unfried Vietnamese rice paper rolls packed with poached shrimp, soft rice vermicelli, crisp lettuce, cucumber, and a fistful of mint, cilantro, and Thai basil — served with a creamy hoisin-peanut dipping sauce. Cool, light, and built for hot days.

<details><summary>📋 Ingredients & steps</summary>

**Ingredients**

- **Rice paper wrappers** — 12 round rice paper wrappers (banh trang), ~22cm
- **Rice vermicelli** — dried rice vermicelli (bun)
- **Shrimp (frozen)** — medium shrimp, poached and halved lengthwise
- **Lettuce** — soft green/butter lettuce, ribs removed
- **Cucumber** — cut into thin matchsticks
- **Carrot** — julienned
- **Fresh mint** — 0.5 tbsp
- **Fresh cilantro** — 0.5 tbsp
- **Thai basil** — 0.5 bag _(optional)_
- **Scallion** — green tops, optional for tying _(optional)_
- **Hoisin sauce** — base of the peanut dipping sauce
- **Peanut butter** — smooth, thins and enriches the sauce
- **Garlic** — 1 clove
- **Lime** — 1 lime
- **Sriracha** — to taste in the dipping sauce _(optional)_
- **Peanuts** — roasted, crushed for garnish on the sauce
- **Sugar** — 0.05 tbsp
- **Salt** — 0.05 tsp

**Steps**

1. Bring a small pot of lightly salted water to a boil. Drop in the shrimp and poach 2-3 minutes until just pink and curled, then transfer to ice water. Drain, peel, and slice each shrimp in half lengthwise so they lie flat; set aside cut-side up.
2. Cook the rice vermicelli in the same pot per package directions (3-5 minutes) until tender but not mushy. Drain, rinse under cold water to stop cooking and remove starch, then drain well and loosen into a bowl.
3. Make the dipping sauce: simmer the hoisin, peanut butter, grated garlic, sugar, a squeeze of lime, and a splash of water in a small pan over low heat, whisking, until smooth and pourable (1-2 minutes). Stir in sriracha to taste, pour into a bowl, and top with crushed peanuts.
4. Prep the fillings into separate piles: lettuce torn into wrapper-sized pieces, cucumber and carrot in fine matchsticks, and the mint, cilantro, and Thai basil leaves picked.
5. Fill a wide shallow dish or pie plate with warm water. Dip one rice paper wrapper in for 5-10 seconds until just pliable — it will keep softening on the board, so don't oversoak — and lay it flat on a clean damp surface.
6. On the lower third of the wrapper, layer a piece of lettuce, a small mound of vermicelli, some cucumber, carrot, and a generous pinch of herbs. Keep the pile compact and leave a clear border on the sides.
7. Lay 3 shrimp halves cut-side down in a neat row on the bare strip just above the filling, so they show through the finished roll.
8. Fold the bottom edge up over the filling, fold in both sides, then roll up snugly and firmly to the shrimp, pressing as you go so the roll seals on itself. Repeat with the remaining wrappers.
9. Serve immediately, whole or halved on the diagonal, with the peanut dipping sauce. If holding, cover with a damp towel so the wrappers don't dry out and stick.

</details>
